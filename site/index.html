Where does your voice go?

With most AI notetakers, a recording of you takes a long trip before you get notes back. With Oatmeal it takes a short one.

A typical cloud notetaker

  1. Your microphone
  2. Their app
  3. The internet
  4. Their servers
  5. Their AI model
  6. Your account on their site

Two tracks in. One conversation out.

Oatmeal records your microphone and your Mac’s audio as separate tracks. Your mic is always You. The other side is split by voice into Speaker 1, Speaker 2 and so on, then everything is put back in order.

It tells voices apart but doesn’t know names. If speaker separation can’t run, you still get the whole transcript with the other side labelled Them.

What Oatmeal leaves out

  • Send a bot to your meetingIt listens through your Mac, like you do.
  • Make you sign upThere’s no account to create, and no server to log in to.
  • Upload your audioSpeech models run on your Mac’s chip, offline.
  • Write summaries for youA summary only sees the meeting. You have the context.
  • Charge you every monthIt’s free, and the code is on GitHub.

Just files. In a folder. On your Mac.

Every call becomes a Markdown file named by date and time, in ~/Meetings or any folder you choose. No app to export from and nothing to sync.

Search them in Spotlight, keep them in Obsidian, or hand one to your own AI assistant, which already knows your projects better than any meeting bot.

Set up in about five minutes

  1. Install the app

    Download the latest disk image, open it, and drag Oatmeal into Applications.

  2. Download the speech models, once

    Open Terminal and paste this. It fetches about 3.4 GB of models to your Mac. After that, Oatmeal never needs the internet.

    Terminal
    /Applications/Oatmeal.app/Contents/Resources/mr-cli/mr-cli doctor --fetch-model

  3. Allow two permissions

    Oatmeal lives in the menu bar, with no Dock icon. The first time you record, allow Microphone to hear you and System Audio Recording to hear the other side.

More detail is in the full install guide.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. There’s no paid tier, no trial and no account. Oatmeal is open source, and you can build it yourself from the GitHub repository.

Does it work with Zoom, Google Meet and Teams?

Yes, and with anything else that plays audio on your Mac. Oatmeal records your microphone and your Mac’s audio output, so it doesn’t need an integration with any meeting app.

Why Apple Silicon only?

Transcription and speaker separation run on Apple’s MLX framework, which needs an M1 chip or newer. That’s what makes local transcription fast enough to be practical.

Which speech models does it use?

Whisper large-v3-turbo for transcription and NVIDIA Nemotron for telling speakers apart, both running locally on your Mac.

Where are my files?

Transcripts go to ~/Meetings/transcripts/ and audio to ~/Meetings/recordings/. Use Change Storage Location… in the menu to keep them anywhere else.

Do I need permission to record a call?

Often, yes. Many places require consent from some or all participants before a conversation is recorded. You’re responsible for following the law where you are and for telling people you’re recording. See the recording consent notice.
